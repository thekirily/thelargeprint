Organised by what comes in the mail

The government tells you almost everything by letter, on a calendar nobody explains. The book starts with your mailbox, month by month: which letter arrives, the one line on it that matters, and the page that explains it.

January

The tax form from Social Security (mailed January 3–24) and the first payment with the 3.6% raise.

September

By September 30: your plan's Annual Notice of Change. The one letter to read twice.

December

December 7: the last day to change Medicare plans. Your raise letter arrives by mail.

Questions people ask

Is this from Social Security or Medicare?

No. The Large Print is an independent publisher. We quote the government's own pages word for word and give the address of each one, so you can check it, but no agency wrote or endorses the book.

How big is the print?

The text is 18-point, and the key numbers are much bigger. It's set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface the Braille Institute designed for readers with low vision. The page is 8½ × 11 inches.

I don't have a printer. Can I still get it?

Yes. Order the paperback: Amazon prints it and delivers it to your door.

Can I send it to my mother or father?

Yes. On Amazon, choose their address at checkout and mark it as a gift. Many copies are bought this way.

Is the 2027 Medicare Part B premium in it?

Medicare hadn't announced the 2027 Part B premium when the book was checked (3 November 2026). The page shows the 2026 amount and the official projection, with a line to write in the final number, and registered owners get the corrected page by email as soon as it's out.

Does it have the new $6,000 senior deduction?

Yes, with the IRS's own wording, the income limit, the rule for married couples, and the IRS rule that a January 1st birthday counts as the year before.

No. It's the published rules in plain words. For choices about your own plan, taxes or health, the book lists the free, unbiased helpers: your SHIP Medicare counselor, Social Security, and the IRS's free tax help for people 60 and over.
